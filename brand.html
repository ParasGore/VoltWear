01 · Naming

Is "VoltWear"
the right name?

Incumbent
Voltwear

What works: "Volt" is the single most ownable word in the category. It is short, phonetically hard, universally understood, and it says electrical — which is literally the technical differentiator.

What doesn't: the "-wear" suffix caps the company at apparel. It is the most crowded suffix in consumer goods (Athleta, Lululemon-adjacent, Under Armour's "wear" lines, 400+ trademarks). It reads as a product description, not a company. Neuralink is not "BrainWear". WHOOP is not "StrapWear".

Premium score6.4 / 10
Trademark difficultyHigh — crowded class 25 + 9
Category headroomLimited to apparel
VerdictExcellent product name. Weak platform name.
Recommended
Axon

The axon is the fibre that carries an electrical impulse away from a nerve cell. It is the exact biological object this company has recreated in textile: a conductive pathway carrying a body's own signal.

Four letters. Two syllables. Hard consonant open, soft close. Sits naturally beside Oura, Whoop, Neuralink and Tesla. Extends to anything the company ever builds — shorts, bras, suits, an OS, a clinic — because it names the mechanism, not the garment.

Five stronger alternatives

Premium score weights: phonetic hardness (20%), syllable economy (15%), category headroom (25%), trademark viability (20%), and how it reads on a $1,290 aluminium case (20%). Recommendation: build and launch as VOLTWEAR — it is the better product name and pre-orders are already attached to it — while securing AXON as the platform brand for the ecosystem expansion in 2027. The remainder of this system is constructed so that swapping the wordmark does not break a single other asset.

05 · Voice

Speak like the
instrument, not
the influencer.

VOLTWEAR states facts and consequences. It never hypes, never uses an exclamation mark, and never congratulates the user for existing. The tone that earns trust from a cardiologist is the same tone that earns respect from an athlete.

We say"Your left erector is 9% below baseline."
We never say"Crush your goals! 💪"
We say"Rest. The data says so."
We never say"You're on fire today!"
We say"FDA 510(k) pending."
We never say"Medical-grade!" (unqualified)
Five principles
  1. 1. Number first, adjective never.
  2. 2. Every claim carries its evidence or its caveat.
  3. 3. Prescribe, don't describe.
  4. 4. Short sentences. Engineering cadence.
  5. 5. Respect the body. It is not a machine to be hacked.

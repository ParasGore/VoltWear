Design language

The circuit is
the ornament.

Every competing smart garment hides its electronics and apologises for them. We did the opposite. The silver-yarn bus is knitted on the outside of the shell in a serpentine that follows the body's own fascia lines — the same lines a sports-tape therapist would draw.

The result reads three ways at once: a neural arborisation, a PCB fan-out, and a flow diagram of energy leaving the core and arriving at the limbs. At two metres it is a graphic. At twenty centimetres it is engineering.

TRACE DETAIL
Motif 01
Arborisation

Traces branch at a constant 34° like dendrites. Signals divide, never cross — crosstalk-free by geometry, not by shielding.

Motif 02
Fan-out

At the rib dock the bus fans into 31 lanes across 28 mm — a literal PCB escape pattern, knitted rather than etched.

Motif 03
Flow field

Line weight tapers from 2.4 mm at the core to 0.9 mm at the extremities, so the graphic reads as energy dissipating outward.

    Integration method

    Sensor islands are ultrasonically welded to the dielectric film, never stitched — no needle holes, no failure initiation points. The island-and-serpentine architecture means the rigid parts never stretch and the stretchy parts never carry a chip.

    Ultrasonic weldNo through-stitching IPX7 encapsulationField-replaceable hub
